# The Global Commons framework (v0.1)

Working draft from the first COP31 session, Antalya, 11 November 2026. Open for comment: file an issue or a pull request.

## 1. The starting point: the commons already exists

The savannah south of Nairobi looks like empty land. It is an elaborate, working commons, with herders, routes, seasons and rules. The same is true at planetary scale: the ocean, the atmosphere, outer space and genetic resources all run on some set of rules already. Starting from zero would mean reinventing the wheel. **The job is to find where the existing commons is being violated, and strengthen the rules there.**

## 2. What's missing: the rules ladder

Place any commons on its step.

| Step | State | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **No rules.** Use is unregulated. | AI agents interacting freely in shared networks |
| 2 | **Weak rules.** Rules exist but don't protect the resource. | Benefit-sharing for genetic data (the Cali Fund): contributions are voluntary, and almost none have been made |
| 3 | **No payout.** Rules protect, but don't remunerate at the scale the resource supports. | Renewable energy: operating solar and wind assets earn high margins (put at 60–90% EBITDA in the session) that could carry a citizen share, but only discretionary community benefits exist today |

Remuneration is itself a rule. At bottom, every rule is a contract.

## 3. The parts of commons infrastructure

All are needed; none comes first.

| Part | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Boundaries** | What is the commons, and where does it start and stop? |
| **Terms** | What do we mean by it? ("Marine protected area" covers everything from no-take reserves to paper parks.) |
| **Rules** | How may it be used? |
| **Rights** | Who holds claims to it? Rules don't automatically create rights. |
| **Responsibilities** | What must rights-holders do in return? Rights and responsibilities come as a pair. |
| **Remuneration** | Who is paid, how much, and in what? |

## 4. The method

1. **Convene everyone who uses the commons**: current, would-be and potential users, across every season. Include the people who don't want a commons: you learn its boundaries from the other side.
2. **Map it** on a shared base map (Gaia), with data layers, a story layer, and the governance itself visible as a layer.
3. **Identify inliers and outliers.** The inliers hold the norm and become its keepers. The outliers include organised actors who extract quietly while blaming users.
4. **Write the rules as contracts** among the users, with rights and responsibilities paired.
5. **Make them strong enough** to protect the resource, without shutting actors out entirely.
6. **Make them pay out**, in what each community actually values.
7. **Scale what works** into formal regulation: prototype outside the state system, then hand over.

## 5. Design principles

### From Elinor Ostrom

Ostrom's eight design principles for long-enduring commons institutions (*Governing the Commons*, 1990):

1. Clearly defined boundaries
2. Rules that fit local conditions
3. Collective-choice arrangements: those affected by the rules help make them
4. Monitoring by, or accountable to, the users
5. Graduated sanctions
6. Accessible conflict-resolution mechanisms
7. Recognition of the users' right to organise
8. Nested enterprises for larger systems

Ostrom herself warned against treating any list as a panacea. Use these as a checklist. Each community's local rules decide.

### From the COP31 sessions

- **Local first.** Roughly 95% of how a commons is managed depends on place. An AI-assisted framework should give the shared principles *and* the two to five local issues that matter in this place.
- **Permeability.** Rules too strict push activity somewhere ungoverned. Keep actors inside the system where they can be seen, and still catch the cheats.
- **Watch for the antithesis.** Organised actors who want no management will blame users for the "tragedy of the commons" while extracting out of sight. Ostrom's work was the rebuttal to that story.
- **Pay in local value.** For a Maasai herder, cattle are money. Payout schemes must convert into what each community values.
- **Stories are data.** Some of what matters can only be carried by a story. Collect stories alongside measurements.
- **Bankability is the test.** Rules and data are a big step forward, but nothing gets done unless the commons fits into the economic system.
- **Govern the shepherd.** Where AI acts as steward of a commons, the framework needs rules for the AI too: human oversight, audit, and a way to override.

## 6. Where to start

Candidate first commons, to be chosen with the community:

- **Medicine from biodiversity.** Half or more of medicines trace to nature. A global benefit-sharing rule exists but is failing at step 2.
- **A marine protected area**, with the Ocean Climate Fund (Azores or Bermuda).
- **A renewable-energy citizen share**, piloted on operating solar and wind assets.
- **AI agents.** A commons at step 1, with a billion or more members expected within a year.
