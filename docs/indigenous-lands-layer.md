# Indigenous and community lands: a data layer for the Global Commons

*In design, 16 November 2026. Started at the Hub Culture Climate Campus, COP31 Antalya, after a session on the Tropical Forest Forever Facility (TFFF) raised the need for a global map of Indigenous land claims. Nothing here is built yet; this note sets out the design for the people whose lands these are to shape.*

## Why

The TFFF is meant to pay tropical forest countries for standing forest, and at least 20% of its payments are committed to Indigenous Peoples and local communities. Those payments flow through national governments, which decide how, and whether, they reach communities. To see whether the money reaches the people who keep the forest, you need to know where their lands are.

Maps already exist. **LandMark**, the Global Platform of Indigenous and Community Lands, has been mapping collectively held lands since 2015, and publishes its data under CC BY 4.0. What is missing is the link between those lands and the money, rules and evidence that the commons tracks: payments promised and paid, claims that settle on independent evidence, and governance the communities control.

## What the layer would be

A Gaia layer that shows Indigenous and community lands **next to** the other commons layers: forests, blue carbon sites, marine protected areas and payment flows. It is built on three rules:

1. **Link, don't copy.** Where LandMark or a community's own map already holds the data, the layer links to it with attribution, and shows it as its source publishes it. We don't build a rival map.
2. **Nothing without consent.** Boundaries a community contributes are published only with its free, prior and informed consent, at the level of detail it chooses: full boundary, indicative area, or not on the public map at all. Some communities face danger if their lands are exposed; the default for anything new is not to publish.
3. **The data stays theirs.** The CARE principles for Indigenous data governance (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) are written into the layer's rules. A community can correct, restrict or withdraw its data at any time.

And one reading rule, which LandMark also states: **the absence of data does not mean the absence of Indigenous or community land.**

## What each land record would hold

- **Who:** the people or community, in the name they use, and who speaks for them on this record.
- **Where:** a link to the source map, and a boundary or indicative area only when consented.
- **Status:** acknowledged by government, claimed but not acknowledged, or contested, each with its evidence.
- **Payments:** what was promised to the community (TFFF, carbon, benefit-sharing) and what reached it, each with its source, the same as the blue carbon registry.
- **Claims:** the same evidence rule as the rest of the commons. A claim settles only when two independent lines of evidence support it, and the community's own testimony is one of them.
- **Consent:** whose consent covers what is shown, and for what use.

## How it connects

- **TFFF payments.** Where a country reports how its 20% is spent, the layer can show which territories it reached, so a gap between promise and payment becomes visible.
- **Blue carbon and marine protection.** Coastal territories sit next to blue carbon sites and marine protected areas on the same map cells.
- **Protection from extraction.** No DIRT territory or other value layer may launch over Indigenous or community land in the layer without that community's consent. This extends the rule DIRT already applies to registered commons.
- **Self-governance.** A people or community that wants to can run its own community on Cloud Mirror, with its own charter, guardians and fund, and link it to its lands on the map. That is always its choice, never a default.

## Questions for Indigenous partners

1. Who should govern the layer itself, and how is that council chosen?
2. What should never be on a public map?
3. How should LandMark, national registries and community maps be weighed when they disagree?
4. Which territories, and which TFFF countries, should come first?
5. Would any community want a value layer or its own Cloud Mirror community, and on what terms?

## First steps

1. Share this note with Indigenous partners and the LandMark team, and change it with them.
2. Agree how the layer links to LandMark's published data, with attribution.
3. Pilot with one territory whose people want to take part, ideally in a TFFF country.
