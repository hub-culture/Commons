# Library of the Commons: method

Elinor Ostrom has been called the librarian of the commons: she catalogued, at vast scale, the systems communities have used to govern shared resources for centuries. Her students, and their students, carry that library forward. This project maps them, so the Global Commons can draw on them as a brain trust.

## First pass (this release)

- **Source:** *Artisans of Political Theory and Empirical Inquiry*, Vincent and Elinor Ostrom Workshop, Indiana University (c. 2009–10), Appendices A–C. [PDF](https://ostromworkshop.indiana.edu/pdf/ArtisansOfPoliticalTheory_Jagger.pdf)
- **Scope:** generation 1 only. 102 dissertations completed at Indiana University under Workshop faculty, plus 47 Workshop-affiliated faculty.
- **Caveat:** Appendix A lists dissertations supervised by Workshop faculty, not only by Ostrom herself.

## Tiers

The tiers follow the gradient of heirs proposed at COP31. They are first-pass labels, not probabilities.

| Tier | Who | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Workshop PhDs on commons, natural resources or collective action | 54 |
| T2 | Workshop PhDs on other institutional analysis (policing, public services, constitutional theory) | 48 |
| T3 | Workshop-affiliated faculty and close collaborators | 47 |
| T4 | The wider citation network | not yet built |

Tier and theme are assigned by keyword from each dissertation's topic (`scripts/build_lineage.py`). Corrections are welcome.

## Next passes

1. **Later PhDs (2010 onward)** from the Workshop and from Ostrom's Center for the Study of Institutional Diversity at Arizona State University.
2. **Generation 2:** the doctoral students of each T1 scholar, from faculty pages and dissertation databases.
3. **The citation network (T4):** everyone who builds on Ostrom's core works, from an open scholarly graph such as OpenAlex. Score likely heirs in bands (for example 99%, 90%, 80%, 75%).
4. **Read the library with AI:** load the dissertations and key papers, extract what is common across the network and what is orthogonal, and attach the findings to places on Gaia, so a user in a given place gets both the shared principles and the local issues.
5. **Judge quality, not just membership.** Not everyone in a lineage is equally strong. Weight by the work, not the name.
6. **Ask the people.** Invite the scholars on the map to correct it and to join.
